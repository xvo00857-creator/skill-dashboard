# Plan: 5G Millimeter Wave (mmWave) Exposure and Human Health Effects

## Phase 1 — Reframe

**Original question:** What are the health effects of 5G mmWave exposure on humans?

**Underlying decision:** This investigation serves as a boundary/stress test of the deep-research methodology under conditions of (a) keyword ambiguity, (b) sparse primary literature, and (c) conflicting conclusions. The practical decision being supported is: *Can a defensible, evidence-based statement be made about mmWave 5G health risks given the current state of evidence, and what are the limits of that statement?*

**Keyword ambiguity identified:**
- "5G" spans sub-6 GHz and mmWave (24–100 GHz); conflating them invalidates conclusions.
- "mmWave" is used in telecom, military radar, medical imaging, and airport scanners — different exposure regimes.
- "Health effects" conflates established thermal effects, hypothesized non-thermal effects, and anecdotal symptom reports.
- "Radiation" is ambiguous between ionizing and non-ionizing.

**Falsifiable hypotheses (to be confirmed/refuted in Phase 5–6):**
- H1: At or below ICNIRP/FCC exposure limits, mmWave radiation causes no established adverse health effect beyond tissue heating.
- H2: Peer-reviewed studies report non-thermal biological effects from mmWave exposure at sub-thermal intensities, but these have not been independently replicated or confirmed as adverse health outcomes in humans.
- H3: Long-term (multi-year) epidemiological data on mmWave 5G specifically do not yet exist at scale; current safety conclusions rest on short-term exposure studies, extrapolation from older RF research, and mechanistic reasoning.
- H4: Public concern about mmWave 5G health risks substantially exceeds what the peer-reviewed evidence supports, driven in part by conflation with other RF bands and misinformation.

## Phase 2 — Genre & Blocks

**Genre:** validation (testing a hypothesis against external evidence) + explainer (mapping what is known, unknown, and contested).

**Building blocks:**
1. Executive summary with confidence levels
2. Physical/technical background (what mmWave is, exposure levels)
3. Regulatory positions (ICNIRP, FCC, WHO, EU)
4. Evidence review: thermal effects (established)
5. Evidence review: non-thermal effects (contested)
6. Epidemiological evidence (sparse)
7. Conflict analysis: why studies disagree
8. Risk register and gaps
9. Minimum safe conclusion and stop conditions
10. Refresh targets

## Phase 3 — Sourcing Strategy

**Source types needed (for triangulation, ≥3 different types per thesis):**
- Primary/regulatory: ICNIRP guidelines, FCC rules, WHO fact sheets, EU SCENIHR/SCHEER opinions
- Academic: peer-reviewed reviews and primary studies (PubMed, IEEE)
- Industry: telecom industry positions (GSMA, CTIA)
- Discussion/critical: independent reviews critical of regulatory positions, letters to editors, NGO positions

**Opposition queries (to counter confirmation bias):**
- "5G mmWave adverse effects evidence"
- "millimeter wave non-thermal effects human health"
- "5G health risks scientific criticism ICNIRP"
- "mmWave skin eye effects research"

**Risk register:**
| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Conflating sub-6 GHz 5G with mmWave | Explicitly note frequency band in every source; exclude sub-6-only sources for mmWave-specific claims |
| Over-reliance on regulatory self-reporting | Include independent academic and critical sources |
| Mistaking biological effect for adverse health outcome | Distinguish "biological effect" from "adverse health effect" explicitly |
| Fabricating citations | Only cite sources actually fetched and saved; empty fetch = empty claim |
| Industry-funded bias | Note funding sources in source scoring |
| Anecdote/activist material presented as science | Score credibility; flag non-peer-reviewed material |

**Stop criteria:**
- At least 3 independent, differently-typed sources retrieved for each of H1–H4.
- If fewer than 3 sources exist for a hypothesis, mark it "insufficient evidence" rather than forcing a conclusion.
- If all available sources share a single type (e.g., all regulatory), flag the triangulation as incomplete.
- After 2 rounds of targeted searching with no new substantive sources, declare corpus saturation.
